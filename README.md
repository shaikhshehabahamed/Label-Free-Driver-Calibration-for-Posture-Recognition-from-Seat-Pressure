# Label-Free Driver Calibration for Posture Recognition from Seat Pressure

A seat pressure mat can show how a driver sits. A model that learns from some drivers often fails on a new driver. We measure this gap on an open dataset of 22 drivers and 20 postures. A random forest reaches 93.3% accuracy when the test driver also appears in training, but only 18.7% for a new driver. Driver identity explains most of the variance in the pressure maps. We test a calibration that needs no posture labels. It subtracts the mean of the new driver’s own recent pressure frames from every frame. Because every driver in the dataset follows one fixed posture order, we shuffle that order. With a linear classifier, a 60-second window raises accuracy by 6.1 percentage points (95% confidence interval 2.6 to 9.5), and a 120-second window raises it by 8.0 points. An independent reimplementation reproduces these gains. The gain depends on how many different postures the window covers, not on how long it lasts. Five postures of 2 seconds help more than one posture of 10 seconds. On causal windows, three published alignment methods add about 2 points at most to mean subtraction. A convolutional network gains 5.0 points from the same step. In boosted trees, pressure adds 15.3 points to elapsed time alone. The calibration gain is unclear for boosted trees, and we study one dataset. A short calibration that spans several postures can help seat pressure models serve new drivers.
